Why Vane Precision Matters in Transmission Oil Pumps for Mild Hybrid Vehicles

Aug 24, 2026

Learn why dimensional control, material selection, heat treatment and inspection matter when developing precision transmission oil pump vanes for mild hybrid electric vehicle applications.

Why Vane Precision Matters in Transmission Oil Pumps for Mild Hybrid Vehicles

As mild hybrid electric vehicles (MHEVs) become part of the transition toward more efficient powertrains, transmission systems must support changing operating conditions without compromising lubrication, cooling or drivability. These requirements place greater attention on the components inside the oil pump, including the small precision vanes that move within the pumping chamber.

A vane is a relatively small component, but its dimensional stability, surface condition and ability to move consistently can influence the operation of the complete vane-type pump. For engineers and purchasing teams developing MHEV transmission applications, selecting a capable vane manufacturing partner is therefore an important part of supplier qualification.

The role of a vane in a transmission oil pump

In a vane-type transmission oil pump, the vane participates in the pumping action as the rotor turns. Together with the rotor, stator, side plates and pump housing, the vanes help form sealed chambers. As the chamber volume changes during rotation, oil can be drawn in and discharged to support the intended pump function.

The vane must move within its rotor slot while maintaining the clearances and surface conditions defined by the pump design. If a component is outside the approved dimensional or material requirements, it may affect movement, sealing behaviour, wear characteristics or consistency of oil delivery. For this reason, vane manufacturing cannot be evaluated only by nominal length and width. The component must be considered as part of a complete pump system.

Why MHEV operating cycles increase the need for consistency

MHEV systems can introduce repeated changes between engine-on, engine-off and assisted operating states. Depending on the transmission architecture, these changes may place emphasis on consistent lubrication and reliable fluid management across the intended duty cycle.

This does not mean that the vane alone determines the performance of an MHEV transmission oil pump. Instead, it means that the vane should be developed and validated together with the rotor, stator, side plates, oil specification, temperature range and operating conditions. A repeatable manufacturing process helps the pump designer maintain the relationship between these parts from prototype through volume production.

For an automotive component supplier, the relevant questions include whether the supplier can control dimensional variation, maintain material consistency, document heat treatment and grinding processes, and provide inspection results against the customer-approved drawing.

Material selection should follow the pump design

Material selection for a transmission oil pump vane depends on the intended load, temperature, oil environment, mating components, wear requirements and validation criteria. A material that performs well in one pump design may not be the appropriate choice for another.

MOUNTOP PRECISION publicly lists SKH51, M2 and high-speed steel among its vane material options, together with a published hardness range of HRC 60–65. These options can provide a starting point for engineering discussion, but the final material and heat-treatment route should be confirmed against the customer’s drawing and application requirements.

A useful supplier review should address more than the material name. It should also clarify the applicable material standard, heat-treatment method, hardness measurement method, dimensional inspection plan and traceability of production lots. These details help the customer connect material performance with the complete pump validation process.

Dimensional control is more than a number

Transmission oil pump vanes are often manufactured to tight dimensional requirements because the vane operates within a compact mechanism. However, the meaning of “precision” must be defined clearly. It may refer to a particular thickness, width, length, profile, parallelism, flatness or another drawing characteristic.

MOUNTOP’s product information indicates a precision range of 0.003–0.005 mm and states that custom-made specifications are acceptable. The company also presents reference dimensions for several transmission oil pump vane configurations. These dimensions should be treated as reference information for engineering discussion, not as universal replacement dimensions for every transmission or pump.

The correct manufacturing approach is to review the customer’s drawing, tolerance stack-up, mating surfaces and inspection requirements before confirming production feasibility. For new MHEV programs, early drawing review can identify potential issues before tooling, sampling or volume production begins.

Manufacturing processes that support repeatable vanes

Precision vane production may involve several connected processes. Heat treatment establishes the required material condition. Grinding controls critical dimensions and surface characteristics. Cleaning helps manage contamination risks before assembly. Measurement and sorting provide information about conformance and process stability.

MOUNTOP’s company information publicly shows heat-treatment and tempering furnaces, ultrasonic cleaning equipment, rough and precision grinding equipment, and a precision automatic measuring and sorting machine. The relevance of this equipment is not simply that it exists; the key question is how each process is controlled and connected to the customer’s quality plan.

For an automotive program, a supplier discussion should cover process flow, inspection frequency, measurement capability, lot traceability, non-conformance handling, sample approval and engineering-change control. These controls help the customer evaluate whether the supplier can support both prototype development and repeat production.

A practical checklist for selecting a vane supplier

Before approving a transmission oil pump vane supplier for an MHEV-related project, engineering and purchasing teams should consider the following questions:
Qualification question Why it matters
Can the supplier manufacture to customer drawings and tolerances? Confirms build-to-print readiness.
Which materials and hardness ranges are available? Connects material selection with the pump’s operating environment.
How are heat treatment and grinding controlled? Supports dimensional and material consistency.
Which characteristics are measured, and are records available? Makes acceptance criteria auditable.
Can the supplier support samples and first-article inspection? Reduces development risk before production release.
Can the supplier manage engineering changes and repeat orders? Tests long-term program suitability.

The answers should be supported by process documentation, inspection records and, where required, customer-specific validation. A supplier’s ability to discuss these topics in engineering terms is often as important as its equipment list.

Positioning a precision vane partner for MHEV programs

For pump manufacturers and automotive suppliers, a precision vane partner can contribute value before mass production begins. A structured drawing review can clarify material, hardness, dimensions, tolerances, inspection points and expected production volume. It can also establish whether the proposed component should be manufactured strictly to customer specifications or developed through a more collaborative engineering process.

MOUNTOP PRECISION positions itself as a manufacturer of transmission oil pump vanes and other precision vane products. Its public product information includes material options, custom-made specifications and reference dimensions for transmission oil pump applications. For MHEV-related projects, the next practical step is to review the customer’s drawing and operating requirements rather than assume that a standard dimension is universally applicable.

Conclusion

The reliability of an MHEV transmission oil pump depends on the complete pump design and its validation. Within that system, the vane must move consistently and meet the dimensional, material and surface requirements defined by the application.

For this reason, automotive customers should evaluate vane suppliers on more than price and nominal dimensions. Material knowledge, heat-treatment control, precision grinding, inspection capability and engineering responsiveness all contribute to a lower-risk supply chain.

MHEV vs. Full Hybrid: Customization Priorities

Traditional ICE vehicles use vane pumps for consistent hydraulic pressure in automatic transmissions, where sliding vanes maintain oil volume under high RPMs. MHEVs, such as BMW X5/X6 48V models, intensify wear through rapid engine restarts, demanding custom vanes: thicknesses of 1.24-2mm, widths 8-9mm, lengths 18-22mm, and tolerances as tight as 3μ. (mountop-tw.com)

Full hybrids (HEVs) like Toyota Prius integrate electric oil pumps (EOP) for engine-off operation, shifting vane focus to cavitation resistance. MHEVs, however, lean on mechanical vanes for primary pressure, prioritizing wear resistance over electric backups.

Feature MHEV (Mild Hybrid) Full HEV
Pump Reliance Custom mechanical vane (primary) Mechanical + EOP auxiliary
Vane Stressors Start-stop cycles, high-frequency restarts Prolonged engine-off pressure
Custom Needs Low leakage (3μ tolerance), anti-wear alloys Quick-response, anti-cavitation profiles (jtekt.co.jp)
Efficiency Impact 10-15% fuel savings via durability EV-mode dominance

These specs minimize aeration and boost volumetric efficiency by 15-20%, a critical edge in fuel economy regulations.

5-Step Guide to Sourcing Custom Vanes

Procurement teams facing supply crunches can spec custom vanes systematically:

  1. Define Specs: Target 3μ tolerances and MHEV-specific dimensions (e.g., 1.24-2mm thickness).

  2. Durability Validation: Simulate 300,000+ start-stop cycles.

  3. Material Optimization: Select anti-aeration composites for low-temp performance.

  4. Lead Time Audit: Prioritize suppliers with <12-week prototyping (Mountop excels here).

  5. Pilot Testing: Integrate into AT benches like GA8HP for real-world metrics.

This approach mitigates risks amid global chip shortages and electrification mandates.
